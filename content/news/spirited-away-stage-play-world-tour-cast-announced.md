# Spirited Away Stage Play World Tour Cast Revealed—Fans Rushing to Book!

The announcement is shaking the anime‑theatre world: the globally‑anticipated Spirited Away stage adaptation has just unveiled its full cast for the upcoming world tour.  With three powerhouse actresses slated to take on the beloved Chihiro role and a lineup of fresh talent, fans everywhere are scrambling to secure seats in Toronto, Los Angeles, London, Taipei, and Tokyo.  This isn’t just another adaptation; it’s a living, breathing celebration of one of Studio Ghibli’s most iconic stories, and the buzz is already off the charts.

## What We Know

- **Chihiro’s Triple‑Star Lineup**: Kanna Hashimoto, Mone Kamishiraishi, and Momoko Fukuchi will all portray Chihiro in the tour’s run.  Hashimoto and Kamishiraishi alternated the role during the 2022 first‑run, while Fukuchi joined them in the 2024 run.
- **Understudy**: Marina Mori will serve as the understudy for Chihiro, a role she filled during the 2024‑25 London, 2025 Shanghai, and 2026 Seoul runs.
- **Supporting Cast**: Nagi Inoue (Nogizaka46), Hana Toyoshima, and Kokoro Hirasawa are confirmed members, though their specific roles remain undisclosed.
- **World‑Tour Dates**:
  - **Toronto, Canada** – Princess of Wales Theatre, May 6 – August 15, 2027
  - **Los Angeles, USA** – Ahmanson Theatre, September 2 – October 24, 2027
  - **London, England** – London Coliseum, March 2028 onward
  - **Taipei, Taiwan** – National Theatre, December 16, 2026 – January 31, 2027
  - **Tokyo, Japan** – Tokyo, March – May 2027
- **Creative Team**: Tony‑ and Olivier‑Award‑winning director John Caird (Les Misérables, Nicholas Nickleby, Daddy Long Legs) co‑adapts the production with Maoko Imai.
- **Previous Runs**: The play opened at the Imperial Theatre in 2022, toured Japan, returned for a 2023–24 run, and played the London Coliseum from April to August 2024—winning the Best New Play award at the 25th Annual WhatsOnStage Awards in 2025.
- **Screenings**: GKIDS screened recorded performances of both the 2022 and 2024 Japanese runs in North America in 2023.

## Why Fans Are Excited

The excitement is multi‑layered.  First, the cast brings a blend of seasoned talent and fresh faces.  Hashimoto, known for her work in *Gintama* and *Kaguya‑sama: Love is War*, brings a fierce energy that fans have already praised in the original stage run.  Kamishiraishi, celebrated for *Your Name* and *Chihayafuru*, adds a delicate, introspective nuance, while Fukuchi’s experience in *Wasteful Days of High School Girl* offers a modern, relatable vibe.  The trio’s chemistry was already proven in past performances, and the fact that they’ll all share the role in the upcoming tour feels like a rare treat.

Second, the tour’s global reach is unprecedented for a Studio Ghibli stage adaptation.  Fans in North America, Europe, and Asia now have the chance to experience the magic in person, rather than rely on the recorded GKIDS screenings.  The inclusion of major venues—Princess of Wales Theatre, Ahmanson Theatre, and London Coliseum—signals that the production is not merely a niche event but a major theatrical milestone.

Third, the creative force behind the adaptation is a powerhouse.  John Caird’s track record with *Les Misérables* and *Nicholas Nickleby* assures audiences that the staging will be both visually stunning and emotionally resonant.  Coupled with Maoko Imai’s co‑adaptation, the production promises a fresh, faithful retelling that respects the original film’s spirit while offering new theatrical depth.

The fan community has been buzzing on social media and forums, with many praising the decision to feature multiple actresses for Chihiro—a nod to the character’s growth and the collaborative nature of theatre.  The announcement has already spurred pre‑sales and a flurry of ticket‑reserving activity.

## Background

Spirited Away, directed by Hayao Miyazaki and Studio Ghibli, was once the highest‑grossing Japanese film worldwide until *Demon Slayer – Kimetsu no Yaiba – The Movie: Mugen Train* surpassed it.  The film remains a cultural touchstone, having won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature in 2003—a record only broken in 2024 by *The Boy and the Heron*, another Miyazaki masterpiece.

The stage adaptation, first staged in 2022, has already proven its popularity.  It ran for 102 performances across Japan before expanding to an international tour.  The London run’s Best New Play award and the subsequent GKIDS screenings in North America cemented its status as a cross‑media success.  This new world tour builds on that legacy, bringing the story to new audiences while honoring the original film’s emotional core.
