**Shōya Ishige Joins the Voice Cast of the Highly Anticipated 'Wound & Bandage' Anime – All the Details You Need Now!**

The buzz is already heating up: Shōya Ishige, known for his dynamic vocal performances, has just been announced as the voice of Ginsuke Hatobane in the brand‑new television adaptation of Umihoshi Nanai’s *Wound & Bandage* (Kizuguchi to Hōtai). The announcement came with a fresh teaser that has fans scrambling to dissect every frame. With the series set to debut on January 9, the countdown is already ticking, and the anime community is buzzing with speculation about how Ishige’s voice will bring the complex character of Ginsuke to life.

The promotional clip showcases the series’ signature blend of romance and crime drama, hinting at the quirky dynamic that will unfold between Riyo, the yakuza heir, and her reluctant caretaker, Gō.

## Why Fans Are Excited

The combination of a well‑known voice actor, a seasoned production team, and a fresh manga adaptation has created a perfect storm of hype. Shōya Ishige’s track record—his work on *The Seven Deadly Sins*, *Fate/Grand Order*, and *My Hero Academia*—has earned him a reputation for adding depth to supporting characters. Fans are eager to see how his portrayal of Ginsuke will balance the character’s tough exterior with the hidden vulnerability that the manga hints at.

Moreover, the *Wound & Bandage* manga has already carved out a niche with its unique premise: a yakuza heir who secretly enjoys watching powerful figures in moments of weakness. That blend of dark humor and romance is a rare find, and the anime’s release on late‑night slots (26:00 on ABC/TV Asahi) signals that the studio is targeting mature viewers who appreciate subversive storytelling. The inclusion of popular creators like Miyuki Ishida and Susumu Yamakawa only amplifies the excitement—both have a history of delivering sharp, engaging narratives.

The community buzz is amplified by the fact that the manga’s fifth volume shipped just last month. With the anime’s launch so close, fans are scrambling to finish the source material, hoping to spot Easter eggs and compare the animation’s fidelity to the original art.

## Background

Umihoshi Nanai’s *Wound & Bandage* first appeared on Kodansha’s Comic Days app in August 2024. The story follows Riyo Washizu, the daughter of the leader of a powerful yakuza organization that dominates the Kanto region. Riyo’s life is far from ordinary: she is a child of crime, yet she harbors a peculiar fetish—she thrives on watching strong people falter. When Gō Kiritani, a young subordinate, is assigned as her caretaker, the dynamic is set to shift. Gō expects a docile, obedient child, but Riyo’s unconventional desires throw him into a comedic, yet emotionally charged, reality.

The anime adaptation, produced by J.C. Staff, brings together a director with a strong visual sense (Miyuki Ishida), a writer known for comedic timing (Susumu Yamakawa), and a character designer who can translate manga nuances into animation (Natsuki Ōyama). The cast features Hisako Kanemoto, a veteran voice actress, as Riyo, and Chikahiro Kobayashi as Gō, promising strong performances from the start.

## What's Next

With the premiere slated for January 9, the next logical step for fans is to keep an eye on the official website for episode release schedules. The series will air on ABC and TV Asahi’s late‑night block, with a simultaneous AT‑X broadcast and an ABEMA stream half an hour later. This staggered release strategy is likely designed to maximize viewership across different platforms.

Given the manga’s ongoing serialization and the rapid release of volume five, we can expect the anime to adapt the early arcs closely before potentially catching up with the source material. Fans might anticipate that the first season will cover the initial relationship dynamics between Riyo and Gō, setting the stage for deeper explorations of the yakuza backdrop and Riyo’s fetish in later episodes.

The inclusion of Shōya Ishige as Ginsuke Hatobane suggests that the anime will introduce new characters early on, possibly expanding the cast beyond Riyo and Gō. Ishige’s role could provide a fresh narrative thread, perhaps tying into the yakuza organization’s internal politics or offering a counterbalance to Gō’s more grounded perspective.
