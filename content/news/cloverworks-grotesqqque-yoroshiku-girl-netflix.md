# CloverWorks’ Grotesqqque Film Drops ‘Yoroshiku★Girl’ on Netflix Early—Fans Rush to Watch!

It’s happening right now: the highly‑anticipated omnibus anime film *Grotesqqque*, the brainchild of director‑screenwriter Atsushi Nishigori, is giving fans a taste of its “Yoroshiku★Girl” segment on Netflix Japan before the full movie hits theaters on November 6. The limited‑time window runs from October 23 to November 5, giving a rare early glimpse into a film that has already made waves at the Fantasia International Film Festival in Montreal and in a U.S. premiere hosted by the Japan Society in New York City.

## What We Know

- **Early Stream Window** – The “Yoroshiku★Girl” segment is available on Netflix Japan from October 23 to November 5.
- **Full Film Release** – *Grotesqqque* opens nationwide in Japan on November 6.
- **Film Structure** – It is an omnibus with three distinct stories: “A(E)liens,” “Yoroshiku★Girl” (also known as “4649 Girl”), and “Nocturne: Nuit Grotesque.”
- **Cast Highlights** – The segment features a star‑packed roster: Sara Minami as Stella, Ikumi Hasegawa as Unknown, Saori Hayami as Hikarin, Maaya Uchida as Gorgeous Mika, Chika Anzai as Sword, Miyu Tomita as Chiffon, Rumi Ōkubo as Papico, and Rie Kugimiya as Poyomi.
- **Musical Contributions** – Thirty‑five musical acts contribute to each story. For “Yoroshiku★Girl,” the lineup includes ASCA, Akki‑otoha, Kotoko Satō, Kanon Shizaki, Ayato Shinozaki, JUNNA, Suisō, Ryōsuke Tachibana, Shiina Natsukawa, Iori Noguchi, Shōta Horie, Myuk, MIRI, Rei Yasuda, Yuyoyuppe, Runaar, and Reol.
- **Themes** – The opening theme “Shoot Shoot Shoot” is performed by METALVERSE, while the ending theme is by Haoto with lyrics by momosu momosu.
- **Production** – The film is a joint venture from JOEN, a company formed by Sony’s Aniplex, CloverWorks, Wit Studio, and Shueisha. Producers Yūichi Fukushima (CloverWorks) and Tetsuya Nakatake (Wit Studio) serve as JOEN’s representative directors.
- **Director’s Role** – Atsushi Nishigori directs, writes the screenplay, and designs the characters.
- **Festival History** – *Grotesqqque* had its world premiere at the Fantasia International Film Festival (July 16–August 2) and a U.S. premiere on October 8 in New York City, where Nishigori introduced the film in person.

## Why Fans Are Excited

The early streaming of “Yoroshiku★Girl” has set the anime community abuzz. Fans are racing to Netflix Japan to catch the segment before the window closes, eager to see how Nishigori’s distinct visual style translates into a short format. The segment’s title itself—a clever play on the Japanese reading of 4649 as “Yoroshiku”—has sparked memes and discussions across Twitter, with many fans speculating on the deeper thematic connections to the film’s overarching narrative.

The cast lineup is a dream team for many viewers. Voices like Saori Hayami and Maaya Uchida bring a level of star power that elevates the segment’s emotional stakes. The inclusion of thirty‑five musical acts, ranging from the energetic ASCA to the ethereal METALVERSE, promises a soundtrack that will resonate with both casual listeners and hardcore anime music fans.

Moreover, the film’s release strategy—world premiere, U.S. premiere, early Netflix window, and then a nationwide theatrical opening—has generated a sense of urgency. Fans who missed the festival screenings are now scrambling to experience the story before the theatrical release, hoping to catch any nuances that might be highlighted in the full film.

## Background

*CloverWorks*, known for high‑quality animation in titles like *The Rising of the Shield Hero* and *The Seven Deadly Sins*, has partnered with Aniplex and Wit Studio under the JOEN umbrella for *Grotesqqque*. Director‑screenwriter Atsushi Nishigori, who previously directed *The IDOLM@STER* and *DARLING in the FRANXX*, is renowned for his ability to blend dark themes with striking visuals.

The omnibus format is a bold choice, allowing each of the three stories to explore different worldviews while maintaining a cohesive thematic core. “A(E)liens” delves into extraterrestrial intrigue, “Yoroshiku★Girl” focuses on human‑centric narratives, and “Nocturne: Nuit Grotesque” offers a nocturnal, gothic atmosphere. This structure has been praised for its ambition and narrative risk.

The film’s music, a crucial component of anime storytelling, features contributions from a wide array of artists. METALVERSE’s opening track sets an aggressive tone, while Haoto’s ending song offers a reflective counterpoint. The collaboration with thirty‑five musical acts showcases JOEN’s commitment to integrating diverse musical styles.
