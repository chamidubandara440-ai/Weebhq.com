# Ghost – End of Night Unveils 3 New Cast Members: A Midnight Surge for the Upcoming Anime Film

The buzz in the anime community is louder than ever as the official website for *ghost – end of night* drops three fresh voice‑actor announcements. In a single press release, the production committee revealed Kenjirō Tsuda, Hiroki Yasumoto, and Hiroshi Kamiya will join the already‑promised cast, bringing a new wave of talent to the film’s central characters. This update arrives just weeks before the film’s world premiere at the Annecy International Animation Film Festival, and it’s a signal that the project is on the brink of full‑scale production.

## What We Know

- **Kenjirō Tsuda** will voice **Moby Dick**, a man who becomes fascinated with Neekei’s world.
- **Hiroki Yasumoto** takes on the role of **Karate**, Masu’s childhood friend and a section manager at the observatory where Neekei works.
- **Hiroshi Kamiya** portrays **Itō**, another childhood friend of Masu and the observatory’s mental coach.
- The film’s main cast remains unchanged: Mayu Hotta as Neekei, and Issei Takahashi as Masu.
- **Shingo Natsume** serves as both director and screenwriter, marking his first time helming an original anime film. Natsume is known for *ACCA: 13‑Territory Inspection Dept.*, *One‑Punch Man*, *Sonny Boy*, and *Space Dandy*.
- **Manga creator Natsume Ono** supplies the original character designs, while **Norifumi Kugai** handles the animation character design.
- **Bandai Namco Filmworks** is the distributor, and the theme song will be performed by the alternative rock band **hitsujibungaku**. The band also performs the insert track “Sugar.”
- The film will open in Japan on **February 11, 2027**.
- The film was screened at the **Annecy International Animation Film Festival** (October 16‑19) and at **GKIDS** and **Variety’s Animation Is Film Festival**.
- A manga adaptation by artist **himiko** began serialization in Kadokawa’s *Young Ace* magazine on **August 4**.

## Why Fans Are Excited

The addition of three seasoned voice actors—Tsuda, Yasumoto, and Kamiya—has sent ripples across fan forums. Each actor brings a distinct vocal presence that aligns with the film’s dark, introspective tone. Fans of Tsuda’s previous work anticipate a layered performance for Moby Dick, while Yasumoto’s portrayal of Karate is expected to add a grounded, pragmatic edge to the observatory’s dynamics. Kamiya’s history of voicing complex, supportive characters promises a nuanced portrayal of Itō’s mental coaching role.

Moreover, Shingo Natsume’s involvement as both director and writer is a major draw. His track record of blending satire, action, and philosophical questioning in *One‑Punch Man* and *Space Dandy* suggests that *ghost – end of night* will push the boundaries of contemporary anime storytelling. The fact that Natsume is aiming for a piece that feels “fictional but in tune with our current time” resonates with audiences craving relevance in a rapidly changing media landscape.

The film’s release date—just a few months after its festival premiere—has also fueled anticipation. Fans know that the festival screenings often serve as a litmus test for the film’s quality and potential for international distribution. The involvement of GKIDS and Variety’s Animation Is Film Festival indicates a strategic push toward a global audience.

## Background

*ghost – end of night* is an original feature from Madhouse, a studio renowned for high‑quality animation and bold storytelling. The story follows **Neekei**, a young woman who questions the notion of “real” in a near‑future society where labor is obsolete. Set in a content‑driven world, Neekei’s journey explores the tension between authenticity and societal expectations.

The creative team is a powerhouse of talent: Shingo Natsume, a veteran of *ACCA: 13‑Territory Inspection Dept.*, brings his distinctive narrative voice; Natsume Ono, known for *House of Five Leaves*, provides character designs that balance elegance with psychological depth; and Norifumi Kugai, who has worked on *Sonny Boy*, ensures that the animation design remains faithful to the original vision.

The music, handled by hitsujibungaku, adds another layer of atmospheric depth. Their alternative rock style is expected to complement the film’s moody aesthetic, while the insert song “Sugar” promises a memorable musical moment.

The manga adaptation by **himiko** adds a cross‑media dimension, allowing fans to engage with the story in multiple formats. The serialization in *Young Ace*—a magazine that has launched many successful titles—provides a platform for readers to experience the narrative in a different medium.
