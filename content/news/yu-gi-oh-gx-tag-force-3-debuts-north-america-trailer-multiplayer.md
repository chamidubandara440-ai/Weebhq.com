# Yu‑Gi‑Oh! GX Tag Force 3 Debuts in North America – New Trailer, Multiplayer, 3,800 Cards

**The wait is over, Duelists!** Konami has just released a brand‑new gameplay trailer for the updated Yu‑Gi‑Oh! GX Tag Force 3, and the news is shaking the entire anime‑gaming community. For the first time ever, the 2008 classic that never made it to North America is finally launching on February 16 for Nintendo Switch 2, Nintendo Switch, and PC via Steam. The trailer showcases fresh duels, a partner system, online multiplayer, and even town exploration—features that have been eagerly anticipated by fans who have watched the series for years.

## What We Know

- **Release date**: 16 February 2026 for Switch 2, Switch, and PC.
- **Platforms**: Nintendo Switch 2, Nintendo Switch, Steam (PC).
- **Content**: Updated visuals, full voice‑acting, enhanced dueling and tag dueling with over **3,800 cards**.
- **Gameplay highlights**: Duels, partner system, online multiplayer, town exploration.
- **Physical editions** (Switch 2 & Switch): include three physical Yu‑Gi‑Oh! Trading Card Game cards—**Yubel, Wicked Canon, Hope for the Future**.
- **Digital pre‑orders** (Switch 2 & Switch): include the same digital cards plus a **Winged Kuriboh Duel Mate** for Yu‑Gi‑Oh! Master Duel.
- **Historical context**: The original game launched on PSP in Japan (November 2008) and reached Europe and Australia, but never hit North America. Its predecessors—GX Tag Force (2006) and GX Tag Force 2 (2007)—did receive North American releases.
- **Anime backdrop**: The Yu‑Gi‑Oh! GX anime premiered in 2004, aired on Cartoon Network (2005) and 4Kids TV (2007) in English. Crunchyroll added season 1 in 2015, later adding season 4 with English subtitles. The series is also available on Hulu, Disney+, Amazon Prime Video, and Tubi.
- **20th‑anniversary remaster**: Yu‑Gi‑Oh! Duel Monster GX 20th Remaster began airing on TV Tokyo in April 2025.

## Background

The Yu‑Gi‑Oh! GX Tag Force series began with the 2006 PSP release, followed by GX Tag Force 2 in 2007. These titles were among the first to bring the tag‑duel mechanic—where two players team up against a common opponent—to handheld consoles. While they were well‑received in North America, the third entry never crossed the Atlantic, leaving a gap in the franchise’s console history.

The original GX Tag Force 3, released on the PSP in Japan in November 2008, expanded the tag concept with more cards and refined mechanics. It also introduced town exploration, allowing players to discover new cards and challenges—an element that the new 2026 version promises to preserve and enhance.

The anime itself has had a storied journey. Premiering in 2004, it quickly became a staple of the 2000s anime boom. Its English dubs on Cartoon Network and 4Kids TV helped cement its global fanbase. In 2015, Crunchyroll’s addition of the first season and later seasons with subtitles brought the series to a new generation of viewers. The 20th‑anniversary remaster, airing in 2025, showcased the series’ enduring appeal.
