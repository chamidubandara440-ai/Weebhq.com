# The Witch on the Holy Night Anime Film is Finally Here – Supercell’s Theme Song Sets the Tone

The moment anime fans have been waiting for is finally arriving.  On Tuesday, Crunchyroll streamed a brand‑new trailer for the long‑anticipated *Witch on the Holy Night* film, and the first glimpse of its theme song, **“Yoru to Ao” (Night and Blue)** by the chart‑topping group *Supercell*, has already lit up Twitter.  The announcement comes just 0.2 hours after the news first broke, and the buzz is already off the charts.  With the film slated to hit U.S. and Canadian theaters on **January 29, 2027**, and a Japanese release on **November 20**, the countdown has officially begun.

## What We Know

- **Release dates**: U.S. and Canada – Jan 29 2027; Japan – Nov 20 2026.  The film will be screened in Japanese with English subtitles and an English dub.
- **Streaming**: Crunchyroll’s trailer preview includes the full opening of *“Yoru to Ao.”*
- **Theme song**: “Yoru to Ao” (Night and Blue) performed by *Supercell*.
- **Studio and distribution**: Crunchyroll and Sony Pictures Entertainment are handling the U.S./Canada release.
- **Japanese premiere**: Coincides with the launch of the visual novel’s smartphone version.
- **Cast**: Haruka Tomatsu returns as Aoko Aozaki, Kana Hanazawa as Alice Kuonji, and Yūsuke Kobayashi as Soujyuro Sizuki.
- **Key staff**: Director Masashi Takeuchi; Character Design Moe Tsuzuki; Chief Animation Director Masato Nagamori; Script Production by ufotable; 3D Director Gōchū Satō; Music by Hideyuki Fukasawa; Chief Director Hikaru Kondo.
- **Background**: The original visual novel, set in the late 1980s, follows Aoko Aozaki, a reluctant heir to a magical legacy, as she balances arcane training with student‑council duties while navigating family tensions and new friendships.
- **Franchise reach**: The game has been available on Steam (Dec 2023) and on PS4/Switch in the West (Dec 2022), selling over 150,000 copies worldwide.
- **Nasuverse ties**: Aoko and Tōko Aozaki appear as mentors and employers in *Tsukihime* and *The Garden of Sinners*, respectively.

## Why Fans Are Excited

The combination of a beloved visual novel, a top‑tier animation studio, and a powerhouse music group is a recipe for hype.  *Supercell* is known for their chart‑dominating hits, and their collaboration on the film’s theme instantly raises expectations.  Fans are also thrilled to see the returning voice cast—Tomatsu, Hanazawa, and Kobayashi—re‑enacting their iconic roles, promising a nostalgic yet fresh experience.

Moreover, the film’s release timing is perfect.  The Japanese premiere aligns with the smartphone version launch, giving domestic fans a double treat.  For international audiences, the U.S. and Canada release date falls in the winter movie season, a period that historically sees strong attendance for anime features.  The inclusion of both subtitles and an English dub ensures accessibility across a wide demographic, further fueling the global fanbase.

The fact that the film is produced by **ufotable**, a studio renowned for its high‑quality animation in *Fate/stay night* and *The Garden of Sinners*, only heightens anticipation.  Fans have come to expect lush visuals and meticulous detail from ufotable, and the promise of a 3D‑enhanced aesthetic from Gōchū Satō adds another layer of excitement.

## Background

*Mahōtsukai no Yoru* began as a mid‑90s collaboration between Kinoko Nasu and Takashi Takeuchi—long before the founding of Type‑Moon.  The original novel was a private draft shared only with friends, never officially released.  It eventually evolved into a visual novel in April 2012, featuring graphics by Hirokazu Koyama and a narrative that bridges the loosely connected *Nasuverse*.

The story’s setting in the late 1980s and its focus on Aoko Aozaki’s reluctant embrace of magic resonate with fans of Nasu’s other works.  Aoko’s older sister, Tōko, appears as a mentor in *Tsukihime* and an employer in *The Garden of Sinners*, creating tangible links across the universe.  These intertextual connections have made the franchise a favorite among fans who enjoy tracing character arcs across Type‑Moon titles.

Commercially, the visual novel has proven its appeal.  The PS4 and Switch releases in December 2022 sold more than 150,000 copies worldwide, and the Steam release in December 2023 expanded its reach further.  The upcoming smartphone version, launching on the same day as the Japanese film premiere, signals Type‑Moon’s commitment to accessibility and cross‑platform engagement.

## What's Next

While the source material stops short of announcing additional sequels, the film’s high production values and the release of a smartphone version suggest a continued push to expand the *Witch on the Holy Night* universe.  The fact that the visual novel’s two sequels were planned during its 2012 launch hints at future narrative possibilities that could be explored in subsequent anime installments or expanded game content.

The collaboration with *Supercell* may also pave the way for future musical partnerships.  Given *Supercell*’s track record of producing popular anime themes, fans can anticipate more high‑profile musical collaborations for upcoming Type‑Moon projects.

From a distribution standpoint, Sony Pictures Entertainment’s involvement in the U.S. and Canada release indicates a strategy to broaden the film’s reach beyond traditional anime markets.  The inclusion of both a subtitle track and an English dub positions the film to attract both hardcore anime fans and a wider mainstream audience.

Finally, the film’s release in late 2026/early 2027 aligns with Type‑Moon’s broader release calendar.  As fans have seen with *Tsukihime – A Piece of Blue Glass Moon* (PS4/Switch in August 2021 and English release in June 2024), the studio often staggers releases to maintain momentum.  The upcoming film, therefore, could serve as a launchpad for future Type‑Moon titles, whether they be new visual novels, anime adaptations, or cross‑media collaborations.

## Closing

The *Witch on the Holy Night* film is more than a simple adaptation; it’s a convergence of beloved characters, high‑caliber animation, and a chart‑topper from *Supercell*.  With the U.S. and Canadian release slated for January 29, 2027, and a Japanese premiere that dovetails with the smartphone version, fans worldwide have a clear reason to mark their calendars.  As the trailer’s haunting visuals and the opening notes of *“Yoru to Ao”* echo through streaming platforms, the anticipation is palpable—this is an event that anime fans will not want to miss.
