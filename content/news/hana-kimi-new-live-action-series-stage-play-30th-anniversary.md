# Hana‑Kimi Celebrates 30 Years with a Brand‑New Live‑Action TV Series and Its First Ever Stage Play

It’s a moment that anime and manga fans have been waiting for: *Hana‑Kimi*—the iconic shōjo manga that ran from 1996 to 2004—gets a fresh live‑action television series and, for the first time, a stage production. The announcement, made just hours ago, has set the WeebHQ community abuzz. With a star‑studded cast, a seasoned director, and a release window that lines up with the winter programming slate, the 30th‑anniversary celebration is poised to be a cultural event. If you’re a fan of *Hana‑Kimi*, the anime adaptation, or just love seeing beloved manga brought to life, you won’t want to miss this.

## What We Know

- **Series Details**: An eight‑episode, half‑hour live‑action television series will air on NTV, premiering on **December 5** at 2:30 p.m. (12:30 a.m. EST). It will stream on **TVer** and **Hulu** in Japan, with Hulu also offering a “director’s cut.”
- **Cast**: 
  - *Mizuki Ashiya* – **Hana Kawamura**
  - *Izumi Sano* – **Makoto Hasegawa** (The Rampage)
  - *Shūichi Nakatsu* – **Itsuki Fujiwara** (The Rampage)
  - *Shinji Noe* – **Keigo Itō** (Ryūgūjō)
  - *Kyōgo Sekime* – **Yūki Tomita** (Ryūgūjō)
  - *Taiki Kayashima* – **Miro Takaoka** (Lienel)
  - *Senri Nakao* – **Riku Morita**
  - *Megumi Tennōji Tagright* – **Genosuke**
  - *Minami Nanba Tagright* – **Daigo Kobayashi**
  - *Masao Himejima* – **Tomoki Nishiyama** (Gekidan Exile)
  - *Itsuki Kujō* – **Yūki Sakurai**
  - *Makoto Kagurazaka* – **Hiroto Takahashi**
  - *Keisuke Shibata* – **Shiori Ueno**
- **Direction & Writing**: The series is directed by **Keisuke Shibata** and **Shiori Ueno**, with scripts by **Fumie Koga**, **Yuka Arai**, and **Kazumi Ōki**. Music is composed by **Yōko Tamura** and scored by **Kōji Endō**.
- **Stage Play**: Written and directed by **Shintarō Sugano**, the stage play opens **February 13‑15** in Osaka (after the series ends), then moves to Aichi on **February 18‑19** and Tokyo from **March 13‑22**. Most of the TV cast will reprise their roles.
- **Story Recap** (per Viz Media): Japanese‑American track‑and‑field star **Mizuki** transfers to a Japanese all‑boys’ high school to be near her idol, high jumper **Izumi Sano**. She disguises herself as a boy, only to discover that she and Izumi become roommates. The story follows Mizuki’s attempts to keep her secret while navigating school life, locker room drama, and the reactions of classmates and the school nurse.
- **Franchise History**: The manga originally ran in Hakusensha’s *Hana to Yume* from 1996‑2004. A short continuation appeared in 2011, and a special chapter in 2014. The latest update, *Himitsu – Hana Zakari no Kimi‑tachi e After School*, debuted in 2021. The manga spawned a 2007 live‑action series starring Maki Horikita, a 2011 series featuring AKB48’s Atsuko Maeda, a South Korean TV adaptation, and a 2006 Taiwanese version. The anime adaptation premiered its first episode at Anime NYC on August 23, 2025, and began airing on Tokyo MX on January 4, 2026.

## Background

*Hana‑Kimi* (full title: *Hana Zakari no Kimi‑tachi e*) was a defining work for **Hisaya Nakajo**, whose storytelling blended romance, humor, and subtle commentary on gender roles. The manga’s run in *Hana to Yume* spanned eight years, cementing its place in shōjo history. Subsequent adaptations—two live‑action TV series (2007 and 2011), a South Korean version, a Taiwanese version, and an anime—demonstrated the story’s universal appeal.

The 2025 anime, which premiered at Anime NYC and later on Tokyo MX, was praised for its faithful adaptation and the chemistry between the main characters. Crunchyroll’s simultaneous English subtitle and dub releases broadened its reach, making *Hana‑Kimi* accessible to a global audience.

The new live‑action series is the first of its kind to revisit the original manga’s core narrative while incorporating modern production techniques. The stage play, directed by **Shintarō Sugano**, offers a unique opportunity for fans to experience the story in a live setting—a rarity for manga adaptations.
