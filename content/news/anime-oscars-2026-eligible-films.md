# Anime Storm: 38 Films, Including All You Need Is Kill & Kaguya!, Eligible for 99th Oscars – Fans on Edge

The Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences just dropped a bombshell that will have the anime community buzzing for weeks. On Monday, the Academy announced that a staggering 38 titles are now eligible for consideration in the 99th Annual Academy Awards, and seven of those are Japanese‑produced films that could see their first Oscar nominations. From the action‑packed **All You Need Is Kill** to the heart‑warming **Cosmic Princess Kaguya!**, the list is a testament to how far anime has come in the global cinematic landscape.

## What We Know

* **38 titles** are now eligible for the **Animated Feature** category.
* The list includes **seven Japanese productions**:
  * **All You Need Is Kill** – Director Kenichiro Akimoto, Studio 4°C
  * **Cosmic Princess Kaguya!** – Director Shingo Yamashita, Studio Chromato & Studio Colorido
  * **Grotesqqque** – Director Ryūya Suzuki
  * **Jinsei** – Director Baku Kinoshita, Studio CLAP
  * **The Last Blossom** – Director Shūkō Murase, Studio Sunrise
  * **Mobile Suit Gundam Hathaway: The Sorcery of Nymph Circe** – Director Yūki Igarashi, Studio OUTLINE
  * **The Ribbon Hero** – Director Yūki Igarashi, Studio OUTLINE
* **Other international titles** also qualify: Nintendo’s **The Super Mario Galaxy Movie**, Illumination’s **The Super Mario Galaxy Movie**, and Paramount’s **Avatar Aang: The Last Airbender**.
* Japan’s official submission for the **International Feature** category is **All of a Sudden (Kyū ni Guai ga Waruku Naru)**.
* To fully qualify, some of these films still need to run in at least one commercial theater in one of six major U.S. metro areas for a straight week before December 31.

In a related development, IndieWire reported that **Gods Don’t Give Gifts**, a film entirely generated by AI from director Zack London (aka YouTuber Gossip Goblin), was ruled ineligible for the Best Animated Feature category. The Academy’s Animation Branch Executive Committee apparently found that the film did not meet the Academy’s definition of an animated film.

## Why Fans Are Excited

Anime fans have long watched their favorite series and movies from Japan make inroads into Hollywood, but the prospect of a Japanese film winning an Oscar is a new milestone. The fact that seven Japanese titles are on the same ballot as Hollywood giants like **The Super Mario Galaxy Movie** and **Avatar Aang: The Last Airbender** signals a shift in how the Academy views animated storytelling.

The inclusion of **All You Need Is Kill**, a film that blends high‑octane action with a mind‑bending time loop, and **Cosmic Princess Kaguya!**, a whimsical tale that reimagines the classic *Kaguya‑hime* story, shows the Academy’s willingness to recognize diverse storytelling styles. The community is also thrilled by the presence of long‑running franchises like *Mobile Suit Gundam*, which now appears in the Oscar‑eligible list for the first time.

The buzz is amplified by the fact that the 99th Oscars ceremony is scheduled for **March 14** at the Dolby Theatre, and the Academy will release the shortlist on **December 15** and nominations on **January 21**. Anime fans worldwide are already lining up for the first time to see their beloved characters potentially win the highest honor in film.

## Background

The Academy’s decision comes after a history of increasing recognition for Japanese animation. In 2024, Hayao Miyazaki’s **The Boy and the Heron** won Best Animated Feature at the 96th Oscars, while Takashi Yamazaki’s **Godzilla Minus One** took home Best Visual Effects. More recently, Toei Animation’s short **Magic Candies (Amedama)** earned a nomination for Best Animated Short Film in the 97th Oscars, even though it did not win.

These achievements have built a foundation for the current wave of Oscar‑eligible anime. Studios like Studio 4°C, Studio Colorido, and CloverWorks have consistently pushed creative boundaries, and the Academy’s latest announcement underscores the growing respect for anime’s artistry and narrative depth.

## What’s Next

The next few weeks are critical. Some of the newly eligible films still need to meet the Academy’s theatrical run requirement, which means they will be opening in U.S. theaters before the end of December. Fans should keep an eye on release dates, especially for **All You Need Is Kill** and **Cosmic Princess Kaguya!**, as these openings could influence early buzz.

In the meantime, the Academy’s preliminary voting for select categories will take place from **December 7 to 11**. After that, the shortlist will be announced on **December 15**, followed by the nominations on **January 21**. The anticipation will only grow as the ceremony approaches on **March 14**.

For **Gods Don’t Give Gifts**, the filmmakers have announced an appeal, and the film is slated for a theatrical run starting **December 4**. Even though it was ruled ineligible, the move shows the resilience of creators who push the boundaries of what is considered “animation.”

## Closing

The Academy’s decision to include seven Japanese titles in the 99th Oscars’ animated slate is more than a bureaucratic update—it’s a cultural milestone. Anime fans now have a tangible chance to see their beloved stories honored on the world’s biggest stage. Whether or not any of the 38 films will secure a nomination, the mere fact of their eligibility is a triumph for the medium and a promise that the future of animation will be ever more diverse and inclusive.
