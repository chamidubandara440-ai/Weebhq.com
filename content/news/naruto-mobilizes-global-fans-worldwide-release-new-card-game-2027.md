**Naruto Mobilizes Global Fans: Worldwide Release & New Card Game Set for 2027**

The Naruto universe is expanding faster than ever, and the latest announcements from the *NARUTO: What’s Next?* panel at New York Comic Con are shaking the fandom to its core. In a single, packed session, the panel unveiled that the long‑awaited Naruto Mobile game—so far only playable in Mainland China—will finally roll out to the rest of the world. At the same time, a brand‑new Naruto trading card game, complete with a slick Studio Pierrot trailer, is slated to hit shelves in June 2027. For the first time in years, the franchise’s flagship characters are stepping onto both digital and tabletop arenas, all under the umbrella of a bold new “Project Rasengan” roadmap that promises a steady stream of content through 2029.

## What We Know

- **Worldwide release of Naruto Mobile**: The mobile game, originally launched in Mainland China in 2016, will become globally available after the *NARUTO: What’s Next?* panel announcement on Saturday. More specifics will drop at Jump Festa ’27, scheduled for December 19‑20.
- **Naruto Trading Card Game (TCG)**: Studio Pierrot produced a teaser trailer shown during the panel. The game will officially launch in June 2027, featuring three starter decks centered on Naruto, Sasuke, and Gaara.
- **Demo schedule**: Select TCG stores will host demos from February 5‑19, then February 19‑March 5, followed by a wider demo event from March 5‑31.
- **Project Rasengan**: The overarching initiative announced at the panel aims to chart the future of Naruto and Boruto across anime, manga, games, and other media. The roadmap, unveiled for 2027‑2029, hints at eight projects, with the first four being the Ninku crossover anime, the new TCG, the worldwide mobile release, and a new manga by Masashi Kishimoto.
- **Historical context**: Bandai’s Naruto Collectible Card Game ran from 2006 to 2013, setting the stage for this new iteration.

## Why Fans Are Excited

The announcement hits multiple hot‑buttons for the Naruto community. First, the mobile game’s global release means that fans who have been waiting for years to play the official Naruto experience on their phones can finally do so. The fact that the game has been in Mainland China since 2016 adds a layer of intrigue—what new features will it bring to a worldwide audience?

Second, the new TCG offers a fresh way to engage with the franchise. With starter decks built around iconic characters—Naruto, Sasuke, and Gaara—players can immediately dive into the world of strategic combat. The demo schedule gives fans a chance to test the game in physical stores, creating a tangible bridge between the digital and tabletop worlds.

The buzz is amplified by the Project Rasengan roadmap, which signals a long‑term commitment to expanding the Naruto and Boruto universes. Fans are already speculating about the crossover Ninku anime and the new manga by Kishimoto, and the simultaneous release of a mobile game and a card game feels like a coordinated push to keep the franchise fresh and relevant.

## Background

Naruto, created by Masashi Kishimoto, has been a cultural juggernaut since its debut in the early 2000s. The franchise spans manga, anime, films, merchandise, and a wide array of licensed products. The original Naruto Collectible Card Game, released by Bandai in 2006, ran until 2013 and remains a nostalgic touchstone for many long‑time fans.

The mobile game, first launched in Mainland China in 2016, has been a significant part of the franchise’s digital footprint. Its global release marks a major milestone, bringing the experience to a worldwide audience after years of regional exclusivity.

Project Rasengan, announced during the *NARUTO: What’s Next?* panel, is a comprehensive plan to keep Naruto and Boruto evolving. By mapping out projects from 2027 to 2029, the creators are offering fans a clear view of what’s coming, from new anime crossovers to fresh manga installments.

5. **Community Engagement**: Demo events in physical stores and the global launch will likely spark a wave of community content—streaming sessions, deck‑building guides, and mobile gameplay tutorials.
