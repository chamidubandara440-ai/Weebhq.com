# Tetsuya Chiba’s Final Volume: Hinemosu Notari Nikki Ends After 8 Volumes – Fans React

The world of manga is in a state of shock and reverence as the late Tetsuya Chiba’s autobiographical series *Hinemosu Notari Nikki* is set to conclude with its eighth and final volume on **February 12, 2026**. Shogakukan, the publisher that has carried Chiba’s work for decades, announced that the last compiled book will ship on that date, marking the end of a 10‑year serialization that began in December 2015. The announcement comes just weeks after the manga’s creator, who passed away on October 22, 2026, due to myelodysplastic syndrome, had already put the series on hiatus on September 25 because of poor health. The news is a bittersweet reminder of Chiba’s enduring influence on the industry and the poignancy of his final words.

## What We Know

- **Final Volume Release** – Shogakukan confirmed that the eighth compiled volume of *Hinemosu Notari Nikki* will be the last, shipping on **February 12, 2026**.
- **Commemorative Book** – On the same day, Shogakukan will publish *Gagyō 70‑shūnen Ai to Heiwa to Chiba Tetsuya* (70th Anniversary as a Working Artist: Love, Peace, and Tetsuya Chiba), a commemorative volume tracing Chiba’s 70‑year career.
- **Series Timeline** – The autobiographical short‑essay manga debuted in *Big Comic* in December 2015. The first compiled volume appeared in 2018, with the seventh volume released in November 2025.
- **Hiatus and Passing** – Chiba placed the series on hiatus on September 25, 2026, citing poor health. He died on October 22, 2026, from a blood‑cell cancer called myelodysplastic syndrome.
- **Early Career** – Chiba began his professional manga career at 17 in 1956, publishing his first work while still in high school.
- **Ashita no Joe Legacy** – Chiba’s most famous work, *Ashita no Joe* (Tomorrow’s Joe), ran from 1968 to 1973. The series spawned two TV anime (1970, 1980) and two films (1980, 1981). It was re‑imagined in the 2018 *Megalobox* anime, which credited the original manga as its source. The sequel, *Megalobox 2: Nomad*, premiered in April 2021.
- **English Release** – *Ashita no Joe* received its first official English translation, titled *Ashita no Joe: Fighting for Tomorrow*, in December 2024.
- **Awards and Honors** – In 2022, Chiba was nominated to the Japan Art Academy as one of its first two manga creators, alongside Yoshiharu Tsuge. He served as chair of the Japan Cartoonists Association and was the first manga artist to receive the prestigious Order of Culture award in 2024.

## Why Fans Are Excited

The announcement of the final volume has sparked a wave of emotional responses across social media, forums, and fan communities. For many, the news is a call to honor a master who shaped the very fabric of modern manga. The *Big Comic* Twitter feed, which hosts the official announcement, has seen thousands of likes and retweets as fans share memories of Chiba’s storytelling genius.

- **Legacy Celebration** – Fans are eager to read the concluding volume, hoping it will provide closure to the personal insights Chiba offered over the past decade. The autobiographical format offers a rare glimpse into his creative process, and many feel the final chapters will be a fitting tribute.
- **Commemorative Book** – The simultaneous release of *Gagyō 70‑shūnen Ai to Heiwa to Chiba Tetsuya* is seen as a perfect companion, promising a curated look at Chiba’s 70‑year career, from his early works to his later masterpieces.
- **Re‑engagement with Ashita no Joe** – The news has reignited interest in *Ashita no Joe*, with fans revisiting the original series, the anime adaptations, and the *Megalobox* re‑imagining. The 2024 English release has opened the door for international audiences to experience Chiba’s storytelling.

## Background

Tetsuya Chiba’s influence extends far beyond a single series. He broke new ground by starting his career as a teenager, a rarity in the industry. His work on *Ashita no Joe* is credited with popularizing boxing in Japan, and its themes of perseverance and struggle resonate with generations. The series’ adaptations into multiple media formats—TV anime, films, and later a sci‑fi reinterpretation—demonstrate its versatility and enduring appeal.

Chiba’s recognition by the Japan Art Academy and the Order of Culture award cemented his status as a cultural icon. These honors, typically reserved for artists in more traditional fields, signaled a shift in how manga creators are perceived in Japan’s artistic hierarchy.

The autobiographical nature of *Hinemosu Notari Nikki* is a departure from Chiba’s earlier, more action‑driven works. Instead of fighting rings or futuristic arenas, he turned the page to his own life, offering introspection on his creative journey, personal hardships, and the evolution of the manga industry itself.

2. **Re‑issues and Translations** – The success of the English release of *Ashita no Joe* may prompt further translations of Chiba’s other works, including the final volume of *Hinemosu Notari Nikki*.
3. **Tribute Projects** – Anime studios and publishers may develop tribute specials, manga anthologies, or fan‑made projects celebrating Chiba’s influence, especially given his role in inspiring *Megalobox*.
4. **Academic Studies** – Universities and cultural institutions might host symposiums or retrospectives, given Chiba’s historical significance and the recent honors he received.

The industry will also likely reflect on Chiba’s contributions to the evolving role of manga artists in Japanese culture, especially as the medium continues to gain global traction.
