**The long‑awaited live‑action Hana‑Kimi series has just dropped a brand‑new trailer, revealed key cast additions, and announced a stage play that will hit the stages in February.**

For fans who have been waiting for the manga’s adaptation to finally hit the screen, this is the most exciting update of the year—everything from the opening theme to the exact premiere date is now confirmed. The 30‑second teaser, released on Saturday, gives a tantalizing glimpse of the high‑school drama that will unfold over eight half‑hour episodes, and the newly announced cast promises to bring the beloved characters to life in a way that feels both fresh and faithful.

## What We Know

- **Series Title & Format:** *Hana‑Kimi* (花ざかりの君たちへ), a live‑action television series based on Hisaya Nakajo’s classic shōjo manga. The show will consist of eight 30‑minute episodes.
- **Premiere Date & Time:** The first episode airs on **December 5** at **2:30 p.m.** on NTV.
- **Streaming:** TVer and Hulu will stream the series in Japan starting half an hour after the broadcast. Hulu will also offer a “director’s cut.”
- **New Trailer:** A 30‑second teaser was released, showcasing the new cast and giving a taste of the opening theme.
- **Cast Additions:**
  - **Kei Hosogai** as Hokuto Umeda, the school doctor.
  - **Miu Arai** as Rika Yamashina, Izumi Sano’s team manager.
  - **Ryōko Yūi** as Io Nanba, Minami Nanba’s mother and Umeda’s older sister.
  - **Yūto Adachi** as Shizuki Ashiya, Mizuki’s half‑brother.
- **Opening & Ending Themes:**
  - Opening: CIRRA (10‑member girl group) covers YUI’s “CHE.R.RY.”
  - Ending: Ryūgūjō performs “Suki Dakara” (Because I Like You).
- **Stage Play:** A new stage adaptation will open **February 13‑15** in Osaka, then travel to Aichi (**February 18‑19**) and Tokyo (**March 13‑22**). Shintarō Sugano is writing and directing.
- **Other Cast (already announced):**
  - Hana Kawamura as Mizuki Ashiya
  - Makoto Hasegawa as Izumi Sano
  - Itsuki Fujiwara as Shūichi Nakatsu
  - Keigo Itō as Shinji Noe
  - Yūki Tomita as Kyōgo Sekime
  - Milo Takaoka as Taiki Kayashima
  - Riku Morita as Senri Nakao
  - Genosuke as Megumi Tennōji
  - Daigo Kobayashi as Minami Nanba
  - Tomoki Nishiyama as Masao Himejima
  - Yūki Sakurai as Itsuki Kujō
  - Hiroto Takahashi as Makoto Kagurazaka
  - Keisuke Shibata and Shiori Ueno (NTV) directing.
  - Writers: Fumie Koga, Yuka Arai, Kazumi Ōki. Composer: Yōko Tamura. Music score by Kōji Endō.

## Why Fans Are Excited

The excitement is palpable because Hana‑Kimi is one of the most beloved shōjo titles of the 90s, and the manga’s 2007 live‑action adaptation only scratched the surface. This new version promises a tighter, more modern take on the story of Mizuki Ashiya, a Japanese‑American track star who disguises herself as a boy to attend an all‑boys high school and chase her idol, high‑jumper Izumi Sano.

Fans are thrilled by the cast’s credibility: Kei Hosogai, Miu Arai, Ryōko Yūi, and Yūto Adachi bring strong acting chops that fans expect will honor the emotional depth of the source material. The inclusion of a dedicated stage play also signals the producers’ confidence in the franchise’s longevity—stage adaptations are rare for shōjo manga, and this move shows the story’s appeal extends beyond television.

The music choices also add to the buzz. CIRRA’s cover of YUI’s “CHE.R.RY” instantly evokes nostalgia for the 2000s anime‑pop scene, while Ryūgūjō’s “Suki Dakara” gives a fresh, upbeat tone to the series’ closing credits.

## Background

Hisaya Nakajo’s *Hana‑Kimi* ran in Hakusensha’s *Hana to Yume* magazine from 1996 to 2004, and it quickly became a defining work for the shōjo genre. The story’s blend of comedy, romance, and coming‑of‑age themes has inspired multiple adaptations:

- **2007 live‑action TV series** (Japanese)
- **2011 live‑action series** starring AKB48 idol Atsuko Maeda
- **South Korean and Taiwanese live‑action versions**
- **2006 Taiwanese live‑action series**
- **Anime adaptation**: Episode 1 premiered at Anime NYC on August 23, 2025; it began airing on Tokyo MX on January 4, 2026, and later on BS11, MBS, CBC TV, and RKB. Crunchyroll streamed it worldwide with English subtitles and same‑day dubs.
- **Second anime season** launched on July 1.

This new live‑action series builds on that legacy by bringing a more contemporary aesthetic and a tighter narrative structure to the story, while still staying true to the manga’s core themes of identity, ambition, and friendship.

## What's Next

With the premiere set for December 5, the series will run weekly on Saturdays at 2:30 p.m., concluding around the end of January. Viewers can catch each episode on NTV, then stream it on TVer or Hulu later that day. Hulu’s director’s cut will offer extended scenes for die‑hard fans.

Following the series’ finale, the stage play will begin a month‑long run starting February 13 in Osaka. The play’s script, written and directed by Shintarō Sugano, will feature many of the same actors from the TV series, allowing fans to experience the story in a live setting. After Osaka, the production will move to Aichi on February 18 and Tokyo on March 13, giving fans across Japan a chance to see the adaptation.

While no official word has been released about a second season, the strong fan response to the first season of the anime and the momentum behind this live‑action series suggest that the production team will be closely monitoring ratings and social‑media buzz. If the numbers match the anime’s success, a sequel could be in the works for the following year.
