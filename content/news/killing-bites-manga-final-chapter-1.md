# Killing Bites Manga Drops Final Chapter Part 1 – Fans Celebrate End of Bloodsport Saga

The long‑awaited conclusion to *Killing Bites* has finally arrived. On Friday, Hero’s Inc. published the first part of the final chapter, sending shockwaves through the community and igniting a flurry of speculation about how the series will wrap up its gritty, blood‑soaked narrative.

## What We Know

- **Final chapter released**: Hero’s Inc. released the first part of the final chapter of *Killing Bites* on Friday.
- **Creators**: The manga was created by Kazasa Sumita (artist) and Shinya Murata (writer), who debuted the series in March 2017.
- **Publication history**: The series originally appeared in *Monthly Hero’s* starting in March 2017 and has since migrated to the *Comiplex* website.
- **Collected volumes**: The 27th compiled volume was published on June 29, marking the last volume before the final chapter’s release.
- **Digital availability**: Mangamo has acquired the manga, making it accessible to a broader international audience.
- **Anime adaptation**: Sentai Filmworks licensed the television anime adaptation. The 12‑episode series premiered on the Animeism block on MBS and TBS in January 2018, with an unaired “director’s cut” streaming worldwide on Amazon Prime Video.
- **Plot premise**: The story centers on Yuya Nomoto, who discovers his friends have orchestrated a plot to kidnap a high‑school girl. He is thrust into the underground bloodsport known as *Killing Bites*, where genetically engineered Therianthropes fight for powerful, shadowy factions.

## Background

*Killing Bites* debuted in March 2017, with Kazasa Sumita and Shinya Murata crafting a world where humanity’s darkest impulses are weaponized. The series quickly gained traction for its unique premise: a hidden arena where genetically engineered creatures—Therianthropes—are forced to fight for the amusement and profit of unseen elites.

The manga’s journey from *Monthly Hero’s* to the *Comiplex* website mirrors the broader trend of digital serialization in Japan. By moving online, the series reached a wider audience and benefited from the instant accessibility that fans appreciate.

The anime adaptation, produced by Sentai Filmworks, brought the brutal visuals to life on the Animeism programming block. With 12 episodes, the anime distilled the manga’s core narrative into a concise, high‑energy format. The unaired “director’s cut” on Amazon Prime Video offered a more expansive look at the story, though it remained unreleased in Japan until recently.

Mangamo’s acquisition of the manga further expanded its reach, providing English‑language readers with official translations and ensuring that the series’ legacy extends beyond Japan.
